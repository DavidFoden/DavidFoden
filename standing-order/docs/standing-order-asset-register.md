# Standing Order — Asset Register

*Every asset the game needs, enumerated with a spec, an acceptance criterion and a sourcing route. v0.1.*

**This is a plan, not a commission.** Nothing here is produced until the legibility gate passes, with the exception of the Phase 1 gate content marked ●. The point of writing it now is that when production starts it is execution rather than decision-making.

Sourcing routes: **L** library or stock · **C** commissioned · **S** self-made · **G** AI-generated (see the note at the end) · **P** procedural or in-engine.

---

## 1.2 Life beds — 12 species

**Acceptance: removing the bed is noticeable without being told.** This is the ecology's teaching mechanism and the single most important audio category in the game.

| Species | Chamber(s) | Character | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rock mites | Rubble Stair, The Kiln | Tiny dry skittering, sparse | L |
| Cave rothe ● | Grimhollow | Large ruminants. Lowing, hooves, breath. Herd mass | C |
| Ember beetles | The Ashvents | Metallic clicking, heat-loving, irregular | C |
| Slag crickets | Ember Galleries | Sharp, sparse, echoing in a big room | L |
| Glowslugs ● | The Fungal Deep | Wet, slow, almost inaudible. **The hardest to notice and the most missed** | C |
| Pale grubs | Spore Terraces | Soft mass movement, a shifting carpet | C |
| Cistern shrimp | The Cistern | Faint water-borne ticking | L |
| Crag goats | Dragonspine Ridge | Hooves on scree, occasional call, scattered | L |
| Cave newts | Weeping Stair | Wet slither, small splashes | L |
| Blindfish | The Black Sump | Surface breaks, gulping | L |
| Silt crabs | Drowned Stacks | Shell on stone, clattering | L |
| Gloom bats | The Rookery | Roost chatter, wing rustle, occasional flight | L |

**Design note.** Glowslugs being nearly inaudible is deliberate and slightly cruel: the richest chamber in Deepholt is the one whose emptiness is hardest to hear. A player who hunts it out may not notice until much later.

## 2.2 Cultures — 4

Each needs: silhouette, mark, banner, territory marker, tools, structures. **Acceptance: identifiable at 40 m in firelight, desaturated.**

Colours and marks are specified in the visual identity document. **The structures matter as much as the bodies** — every camp building must read as *misuse of dwarven infrastructure*.

## 2.3 Creatures — 14

12 prey plus 2 apex. **Acceptance: silhouette-identifiable in low light.** Prey need idle, feed, flee and death states. The dragon needs a gaunt variant.

## 2.4 Items — ~22

Food (4) · party tools (7) · dwarven scavenge (6) · tokens (2) · the sluice key (1, and it must look important without looking magical) · trade goods (2).

## 2.5 Interface — 6 screens

Main menu (the plate) · lobby · **the fire** · **the map** · inventory · endings. The fire and the map are the two that must be genuinely good.

## 2.6 Accessibility co-signals — 11

**Not decoration — these are gameplay assets.** Torch bloom at tunnel mouths · ceiling dust · fleeing prey · stripped-chamber dressing · smoke and firelight from camps · camp-gathering visuals · kill remains and cleared ground · water level drop · drying stone · footfall disturbance · promise tokens.

**Build these into chambers from the start.** Retrofitting them is expensive; designing them in is nearly free.

# PART 3 — Content

## 3.1 Voice bible — 6 pages

**Write first.** Sporewardens, Ashfang, Mirelurks, Assayers, Tallow, Ovik. **Acceptance: five lines with the speaker removed, and a reader names who is talking.**

## 3.2 Beats — 40

All specified in the beat library. Write in the order given there: contact → revelation → bands → faith → political → capture → ecological → tools and trade → the Gate.

**Phase 1 ●:** B01, B03, B16, B36, B20.

## 3.3 Dialogue fragments — ~200

A grid: 8 slots × 6 speakers × ~4 variants. **Batch by slot, not by speaker** — write all six refusals in one sitting so you can hear whether the voices differ.

Slots: greet · refuse · agree · grievance · rumour · demand · warn · part.

## 3.4 Prose

4 gospels + the plate (**done**) · 5 endings + epilogue template · 16 chamber descriptions · ~50 item and creature descriptions · the opening's incidental village dialogue.

# PART 4 — Sourcing strategy

**Library first for anything natural.** Drips, wind, stone, water, scree — these exist at high quality and recording them yourself is not a good use of a day a week.

**Commission the things that carry state.** The band audio, the four camp sets, the apex predators, the distinctive life beds, and the two music cues. These are perhaps 20 assets and they are the ones the game cannot work without.

**Self-make foley and interface.** Cheap, iterative, and it needs to match your own animations.

**On AI generation.** Unity's generators are improving but reviews describe world generation as not yet production-ready, and single-asset generation as the strong case. Two constraints specific to you: generated assets carry embedded metadata flagging them as AI generated and you remain responsible for store declarations and usage rights — worth settling as Layernest policy before anything ships. And this game's art direction is silhouette-led and culture-specific, which is exactly where generic generation is weakest.

**Reasonable use:** whitebox props, background clutter, first-pass concept exploration. **Poor use:** the four cultures, the creatures, anything a player identifies by shape.
