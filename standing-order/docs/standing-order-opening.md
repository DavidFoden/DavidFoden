# Standing Order — The First Ten Minutes

*The opening, specified as a sequence. v0.1.*

This is the only part of the game that is identical every time, so it is the only part that can be written as a script. Everything after it is assembled by the simulation.

It is also the highest-stakes content in the project. Ten minutes decides whether a player understands what kind of game this is.

---

# PART 1 — What it must achieve

Six things, in this order, and **none of them may be stated in text**. Every one is taught by a situation.

1. **You are ordinary.** Not chosen, not equipped, not competent.
2. **Sound is how you perceive.** Taught before sight, because sight barely works down here.
3. **Light is carried and it costs something.**
4. **There is no way back.**
5. **You are hungry, and eating means killing something.** This is the ecology entering, disguised as survival.
6. **The world down here is inhabited, and it is frightened.** Overheard, never addressed.

Invariants 1–4 from the master specification are delivered here. Invariant 5 — someone recognises you — comes later in Act I.

---

# PART 2 — What it must not do

- **No tutorial text, no prompts, no objective marker.** If a situation cannot teach it, it is not taught yet.
- **No lore.** Nobody explains dwarves, the ruin, or the prophecy. The player should not know the word "Deepholt" for an hour.
- **No named character.** Tallow and Ovik do not exist yet.
- **No combat.** Nothing attacks the party in the first ten minutes.
- **No camp contact.** Camps are *heard*, never met.
- **No music** until the aftershock, and then only briefly.
- **No character creation after the fall.** It happens in the lobby, before the world loads, so it does not eat the opening.

---

# PART 3 — The sequence

Timings are targets, not rails. A slow party can take twenty minutes and nothing breaks.

### 0:00 — Above

Late afternoon in Marrowing. Ordinary work, ordinary light, no music. The party walks somewhere unremarkable — a field edge, a track, a stile. Two or three lines of nothing conversation.

**Teaching:** movement, and that you are a peasant.

The camera never lingers on anything portentous. There is no shot of a hole, no distant rumble, no bird flying up.

### 0:40 — The tremor

The ground moves once, hard. Then it opens.

**The fall is played, not watched.** You are in control, you have no control, and that is the point.

### 1:00 — Separated

The party lands scattered across a dark chamber, out of sight of each other.

**The first thing every player does is make noise to find their friends.** Calling out is the natural, correct, only action.

**Teaching:** `Listen`, discovered rather than instructed. Players stop, hold, hear a friend, and move toward them. It teaches the game's primary sensor in the first ninety seconds using an instinct nobody has to be told.

It also lays a trap that pays off two hours later: **the safest thing you ever did was shout in the dark**, and by then you will know what noise attracts.

### 2:00 — The light

Reunited, and there is a shaft of daylight from the hole above. Dust turning in it. It looks reachable.

**Players will try to climb.** They must be allowed to try, properly, for as long as they want. Rubble shifts, a route half-works, someone boosts someone.

**Teaching:** carrying, cooperation, and hope. This section exists to make the next one land.

### 4:00 — The aftershock

The ceiling closes. Not a cutscene — it happens while they are working at it, and it should very nearly catch somebody.

Then: dark, dust, and quiet. **The only music cue in the game until the endings**, and it is short.

**Teaching:** there is no way back. Nobody says it.

### 4:30 — Fire

Somebody has a firestriker, or there is old timber and a way to make one. The first fire is small and warm and does not reach the walls.

**Teaching:** light is carried, light is finite, and the dark is bigger than the light. The chamber is not revealed by the fire — a player should understand immediately that they cannot see the room they are standing in.

### 6:00 — Something else is alive

Away in the dark, the life bed of the chamber comes up: small movement, small sounds. Not a threat. **Rock mites, or whatever the landing chamber holds.**

Players will hold `Listen` because they learned it at 1:00. This time it shows them where the animals are.

**Teaching:** the world has animals in it, and `Listen` locates things that are not your friends.

### 7:30 — Hunger

Nobody announces it. Hands begin to shake. Hearing shortens slightly.

**Teaching:** hunger is a body, not a bar — the first and most important information-design lesson in the game.

### 8:00 — The first kill

Small, grim, unglamorous, and completely successful. Four peasants corner something the size of a cat and kill it with a pitchfork, and it feeds them.

**Teaching:** you eat by taking from this place. This is the ecology entering, and the player experiences it as relief rather than consequence. **That is deliberate.** The chamber they strip in hour one is one they will return to in hour four.

### 9:00 — Voices

Far off, through rock: **voices, arguing, frightened, and not human.**

Not language the player can follow. Not directed at them. Whatever happened has clearly happened to somebody else as well, and they are alarmed about it.

The party can move toward it or away. Either is correct. **Nothing is met.**

**Teaching:** invariant 3 — the camps felt it too. The world is not waiting for you and did not notice you arrive.

### 10:00 — Act I proper

The party is fed, lit, trapped, and has heard something. From here the simulation runs and nothing is scripted again.

# PART 4 — The teaching order, and why

**Sound before sight** — because sight barely functions and a player who learns to look first will struggle for hours.

**Cooperation before threat** — the first use of every co-op verb happens when nothing is hunting you.

**Hope before loss** — the climb must be genuinely attempted or the aftershock is just an event.

**Hunger before ecology** — the player must feel eating as survival before they understand it as damage. Reverse this and the whole Act III payoff evaporates.

**Inhabitants heard before seen** — establishes a populated world without spending a first-contact beat.

---

# PART 5 — Party size

**Four players.** As written.

**Two or three.** Identical, fewer landing points. The climb needs to work with two, so the boost must be a two-person action.

**Solo.** The three NPC followers are scattered in exactly the same way and are found the same way. **Do not shorten it** — finding them is how a solo player learns `Listen`, and it is the only sequence where the followers feel like people rather than baggage.

**Drop-in.** A player joining later does not replay this. They arrive with an empty map and an empty chronicle, which is its own strong opening — everything the others know, they do not.

---

# PART 6 — Rules for the section

- **No fail state.** Nothing here can be got wrong. There is no way to die, no way to be trapped, and no way to miss a step.
- **Everything is skippable by walking.** A player who ignores the light and walks into a tunnel at 2:30 is not stopped. The aftershock happens anyway, wherever they are.
- **The chamber is the real Fissure**, not a bespoke set. It has 3 flora and no prey, which is exactly why the party must move to eat. The map is doing narrative work by being what it already is.
- **No dialogue wheel, no choices.** The party talks; the players do not select.
- **Second playthroughs get the same opening.** It is an invariant. The village walk may be shortened, and nothing else.

---

# PART 7 — Failure modes to watch for

Each of these has killed an opening before.

**The tutorial reflex.** Somebody adds "Hold TAB to Listen." The moment that text exists, the discovery at 1:00 is destroyed and the game announces that it will tell you what to do.

**Making the fall a cutscene.** Cheaper to build, and it costs the single most important thing in the sequence — that you were in control and then were not.

**Explaining the aftershock.** A character saying "we're trapped" halves it. Let the silence do it.

**Making the first kill heroic.** It must be small and slightly wretched. If it feels like a hunt, the player is an adventurer and the game has already drifted.

**Front-loading lore.** Somebody will want a title card reading *Deepholt — 400 years sealed*. It is the single most damaging line that could be added to this game.

---

# PART 8 — Test conditions

**O1 — Under two minutes.** From launch to standing in the dark underground. Measure it.

**O2 — Nobody asks what to do.** Five testers, no guidance, none of them asks the facilitator what they are supposed to be doing at any point in the ten minutes.

**O3 — `Listen` is discovered.** At least four of five testers use `Listen` before minute three, without prompting.

**O4 — The climb is attempted.** Every group tries to get out before the aftershock. If they do not, section 2:00 has failed and the aftershock lands flat.

**O5 — The voices land.** Asked afterwards what they heard at the end, testers report something alive and frightened — not "enemies."

**O6 — No lore recall.** Asked what the place is, testers cannot say, and are not bothered by not knowing.

**O7 — Solo parity.** A solo player reaches 10:00 having used `Listen` and eaten, same as a group.
