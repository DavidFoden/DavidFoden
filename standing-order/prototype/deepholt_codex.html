Deepholt

A sealed dwarven biosphere, unattended for four hundred years. Fifteen chambers, four cultures, one broken ceiling. A field guide for the design team.

When the ceiling opens, those who fall are to be conducted to the cistern.

Recovered from a dwarven wall plate. Recopied by illiterates for four centuries and now held, in four differing versions, as scripture. It was an evacuation procedure. The peasants have not been mistaken for messiahs. They have been mistaken for a plumbing callout.

The cavern

The dwarves built cisterns, air shafts, fungus farms and livestock pens, then died or left. The system kept running unattended. Every creature down here descends from dwarven labour, livestock or vermin — all of them fighting over infrastructure none of them can rebuild.

Sporewardens — the Fungal Deep. Devout. The prize chamber and the softest defenders.
Ashfang goblins — the Ashvents. Belligerent, numerous, quick to copy any tool they see.
Mirelurk clan — the Black Sump. Fishers and traders. Sell the prophecy, don't buy it.
Assayers — Dragonspine Ridge. Poor, few, deeply dug in. They have their own god.
The Sealed Gate — dead rock, and the way the dwarves left. Whatever holds it shut has held for four hundred years.
The new fissure — opened by the earthquake. Tick zero. The players fall through it and the equilibrium ends.

The four camps

Each differs in culture, intelligence, creed and taboo. Intelligence is not flavour — it decides how many actions a camp can even see. The dim ones react to hunger. The cunning ones negotiate, extort and betray.

The goblins hunted the ridge too hard, so the dragon starved. Nobody wrote that.

The food webs

Every chamber runs flora, a prey species and sometimes a predator. Camps hunt named animals, not a resource bar. Hunt a chamber out and it collapses — then recovers, if anyone leaves it alone long enough. The party eats from the same web, which is how four peasants wreck an ecosystem simply by being hungry.

When faith breaks

The Sporewardens are the camp players befriend first, which makes them the camp most likely to be disappointed. Faith erodes on specific acts — a taboo broken, a promise unkept, a sacred chamber hunted out — each logged at the time in words the player can't yet read.

When it snaps, fervour suppresses foraging, migration and defence, and multiplies one score: reach the party. They will hunt you while starving, because failed prophecy intensifies belief rather than ending it. The camp splits — hardliners who want you dead, a remnant who insists the sign still holds. That remnant is the only way back.

And a camp that ignores hunger dies. Its hunting grounds go unworked, its neighbours' balance shifts, and the fallout crosses the map. You didn't make an enemy. You doomed them.
